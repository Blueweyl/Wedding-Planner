1. Open the planner

1

Unzip the folder

You downloaded a .zip file. Unzip it before you start — the planner will not save your work properly from inside a zip.

  • Windows: right-click the zip → Extract All… → Extract.
  • Mac: double-click the zip. A folder appears beside it.

Put the unzipped folder somewhere you will not lose it — Documents is ideal. Not Downloads, which people tend to empty.

2

Double-click Evermore-Wedding-Planner.html

It opens in your web browser and you can start straight away.

3

Bookmark it

Once it is open, bookmark the page (Ctrl+D, or ⌘+D on a Mac) so you can get back to it in one click. Keep using that same bookmark, in that same browser — that is where your plan lives.

No installing, no account, no internet needed. There is nothing to set up and nothing to sign into. The planner is a single file that runs inside your browser, on your computer, offline. Nothing you type is ever sent anywhere.

2. Your first few minutes

The planner asks for both of your names and your wedding date, then opens on Home. Work through the tabs in whatever order suits you:

  • Budget — set a planned amount per category, then record what you actually spend. Leave everything at 0 and it simply says "Budget not set".
  • Guests — add names, mark RSVPs, note meals and plus-ones. The counts at the top only ever count confirmed yes guests and their confirmed plus-ones.
  • Timeline — a full checklist, with each phase dated backwards from your wedding day so you can see what is due now and what has slipped.
  • Vendors — contacts, booking status, total cost, deposit, what you have paid, what is still owed, and which payments are due or overdue.
  • Diary and Vows — private notes, and a place to draft your vows with a word count and a spoken-time estimate.
  • Concierge ✦ — your next three actions, worked out from everything above. New in 2.0, and explained in the next section.
  • Settings — your names, your date, your currency, backups, safety copies, the optional AI assistant, and start-over.

The Timeline is yours to shape: add your own tasks at the bottom of any phase, and remove the ones that don't apply to you (there is an Undo straight after).

Everything saves itself as you type. There is no save button, and you will never lose a keystroke to a forgotten one.

3. Your Smart Concierge

The Concierge tab reads your whole plan — payments, RSVPs, budget, checklist, vendors and vows — and tells you, in plain words, what matters most right now. Home shows the same next three steps with a button straight to it.

  • Your next three actions — the most urgent things first: an overdue deposit outranks a florist you haven't found yet.
  • Everything else, by area — payments, RSVPs and guests, budget, deadlines, bookings, and backups.
  • Budget savings — ideas matched to your biggest categories, and what each extra guest costs in catering. The savings figures are a rough guide, not a quote.
  • Ask the Concierge — type a question like "How much do we still owe?" or "Who hasn't RSVPed?" and it answers from your plan.
It works completely offline and sends nothing anywhere. It never changes your plan by itself either. When it offers a shortcut — say, Record the deposit as paid — it shows you exactly what will change and waits for you to confirm. It keeps a safety copy first, and there is an Undo straight after.

Not relevant? Press Not now and that suggestion is hidden for a week.

4. The optional AI assistant

If you like, the Concierge can also use an AI model for personalised advice, writing your vows, drafting emails to vendors, and open questions. This is entirely optional: there is no subscription, nothing to buy from us, and every other part of Evermore works without it.

It uses your own API key from an AI provider (Anthropic Claude is the one we recommend and have tested), so you pay the provider directly for what you use — typically a few cents per answer, and you can set a monthly spending cap with the provider. Before anything is sent, Evermore shows you exactly what will be shared and asks for your OK. Guest names, contact details and your diary are never sent.

Setup takes about five minutes. Open AI-SETUP-GUIDE.html, in this same folder, for the steps.

5. Where your plan is saved

Your plan is stored by the browser you opened it in, on the computer you opened it on. That is what makes it private — there is no server and no account, so nobody but you can see it.

It also means the plan does not follow you around. If you open the planner in a different browser (say you normally use Chrome and one day open it in Edge), or on a different computer, that copy starts empty. To carry your plan across, use a backup file — that is next.

6. Save a backup

This is the one habit worth having. Go to Settings → Backup → Save backup file. A file called evermore-backup-date.json lands in your Downloads folder (the date in the name means you never confuse an old backup with a new one). Keep it somewhere safe: email it to yourself, drop it in your cloud storage, put it on a USB stick.

Do it:

  • after any big planning session;
  • before you clear your browsing data, run a "cleaner" program, or update your computer;
  • before switching browsers or computers;
  • once a month, roughly, because it takes five seconds.
If the planner ever tells you a change was not saved — a red bar at the top of the page — your browser has refused to store it (usually a full disk, or a private window). Nothing you typed is lost from the screen, but it will go when you close the tab. Use Save backup file immediately, then reopen the planner normally and restore it.

Automatic safety copies

Evermore 2.0 also keeps up to three safety copies by itself: once a day, and just before anything big — restoring a backup, starting over, or accepting a change from the Concierge or the AI assistant. You will find them under Settings → Backup, each with a Restore this copy button. They live in the same browser as your plan, so they are a seatbelt, not a replacement for a backup file.

Settings also shows when you last saved a backup, and the Concierge reminds you if it has been more than a month.

7. Restore a backup

  1. Open the planner.
  2. Go to Settings → Backup → Restore from backup.
  3. Pick your backup file. The planner tells you whose plan it is, when it was saved and how many guests and vendors it holds — check that, then confirm.

Restoring replaces everything currently in the planner with the contents of that file. A safety copy of what was there is kept first, and an Undo appears at the top straight after, so the wrong file is a two-second fix. Backups from version 1.0 restore perfectly, and a 2.0 backup still restores into 1.0.

If the file is incomplete or damaged, the planner will say so and change nothing at all. Your current plan is never half-replaced.

8. What can erase your plan

Clearing your browser data can delete your plan. Anything that clears "cookies and site data" for local files will take your plan with it: browser cleanup tools, "clear browsing data" with site data ticked, tune-up or privacy apps, resetting the browser, or a new browser profile. Keep a recent backup file and this is a non-event.

Also worth knowing:

  • Private / Incognito windows forget everything when you close them. Do not plan a wedding in one.
  • Moving or renaming the folder after you start can make the browser treat it as a different page, with an empty plan. If that happens, move it back, or restore your backup.
  • Deleting the .html file removes the planner, though your saved plan stays in the browser until the browser data is cleared.

9. Move to another computer

  1. On the old computer: Settings → Save backup file.
  2. Copy two things to the new computer: the unzipped planner folder, and that backup file.
  3. On the new computer: open Evermore-Wedding-Planner.html, then Settings → Restore from backup and pick the file.

The same three steps work for planning on two computers at once — say one each. Just remember there is no automatic syncing: whoever makes changes saves a backup, and the other person restores it. Two people editing at the same time will overwrite each other, so take turns.

10. Using it on your phone

Yes — on an iPhone, an iPad or an Android phone. Phones cannot run the downloaded file directly, so there is a web address for exactly this:

Open this on your phone
evermore-planner-k4m9x2.netlify.app

It is the same planner, and it still keeps everything on your own phone — no account, nothing sent anywhere. Add it to your home screen and it gets its own icon and opens like an app:

  • iPhone / iPad (Safari): open the link, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): open the link, tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home Screen.

After that first visit it works with no signal at all — on a plane, in a venue basement, anywhere.

Your phone and your computer keep separate plans. There is no syncing, because there is no account and no server — that is the same reason nobody can see your plan. Pick one device as the one you really plan on. To move everything across, save a backup on that device and restore it on the other (sections 6 and 7).

On an iPhone, saving a backup works a little differently

Tapping Save backup file may open the backup in a new tab instead of downloading it. If it does: tap Share → Save to Files, and keep it in iCloud Drive. Restoring then works through Settings → Restore from backup → Browse → the file you saved.

Because of that, if you only plan on a phone, take a backup on a laptop now and then too — it is the easiest place to keep one safely.

11. Export the guest list

Guests → Export CSV saves guest-list-date.csv to your Downloads folder. It opens in Excel, Numbers, Google Sheets or anything else that reads spreadsheets, with a column for the name, the RSVP, the meal, the plus-one, the plus-one's name and how many seats that row needs.

Handy for sending a final headcount to your caterer, or for building a seating chart.

12. Print or save as PDF

Any tab prints from your browser (Ctrl+P, or ⌘+P on a Mac). In the print dialog, choose Save as PDF as the destination instead of a printer, and you have a file to keep or send.

The keepsake is made for this: mark diary entries with "include in keepsake", then use the keepsake view's Print / Save as PDF button. Tick Include our vows in the keepsake on the Vows tab to add them at the end. It prints as clean typeset pages, with the buttons and navigation left out.

13. Recommended browsers

Evermore 2.0 is built and tested on Google Chrome and Microsoft Edge (current versions, both built on Chromium). Those are the safe choice.

It uses nothing exotic, so other current browsers — Firefox, Safari, Brave, Opera — should work too. If anything looks or behaves oddly in one of those, open it in Chrome or Edge instead and it will be fine. Internet Explorer is not supported.

On a phone, use Safari on an iPhone or iPad and Chrome on Android, with the link in section 10. Those are the two that can add it to your home screen properly.

14. Privacy and security

  • Your plan is stored only in your browser on your computer. Evermore has no server, no account, no tracking and no analytics.
  • The planner makes no internet requests at all unless you turn on the optional AI assistant. Even the typefaces are built in. A strict security policy inside the file blocks connections to anywhere except the AI providers listed in Settings.
  • Your AI key is kept only on this computer — and, unless you tick Remember my key, only until you close the tab. It is never put in a backup file.
  • Anything you type, and anything an AI writes, is shown as plain text. It can never run as code in the page.
  • Guest names, meal choices, contact details and your diary are never sent to an AI provider.

15. Troubleshooting

The file opens as code, or in a text editor

Right-click it → Open with → your browser. To make that permanent, use Open with → Choose another app (Windows) or Get Info → Open with (Mac) and set your browser as the default for .html files.

The page is blank

Make sure you unzipped the folder first, and that you are opening Evermore-Wedding-Planner.html rather than the zip. Then refresh the page.

A yellow bar says some damaged fields were reset

Your plan opened, but one or two values in it could not be read (an impossible date, say), so just those were set to a safe blank. Everything else is exactly as you left it. If something important looks wrong, go back to a safety copy or a backup.

A bar says saved data "could not be read"

Something outside the planner damaged what the browser stored. Evermore 2.0 puts that data safely to one side before anything can overwrite it. Restore your latest backup from Settings, and use Download unreadable copy to send the damaged data to support if you would like help recovering it.

The AI assistant says it can't connect

See the troubleshooting section of AI-SETUP-GUIDE.html. The Smart Concierge keeps working either way.

My plan is gone

Check three things: are you in the same browser you started in; are you opening it from the same folder; and have you cleared browsing data recently? If none of those explains it, restore your most recent backup file from Settings → Restore from backup.

Settings will not accept my change

The planner needs both names and a wedding date that has not already passed, so it refuses an empty name or a past date and tells you why, rather than saving something broken. Your previous details stay in place until you enter something it can use.

A vendor row says "Check this"

That means the booking status and the money recorded disagree — for example "Paid in Full" with nothing paid. Nothing has been changed for you; update whichever of the two is out of date.

Nothing downloads when I save a backup or a CSV

Look for a blocked-download notice in your browser's address bar and allow it, then try again. Also check your Downloads folder — the file often arrives without any visible fuss.

The date field behaves strangely

Date fields are drawn by your browser, not by the planner, so they look and work slightly differently in each one. Typing the date in the format your browser shows always works.
